The Power Paradox: Men's Mental Health Stigma and Leadership

By Dr. Alina Schulhofer

Despite growing awareness, men’s mental health often remains a taboo subject, overshadowed by ideals of unwavering strength and resilience. Male vulnerability is widely stigmatized, which often leads to reluctance in seeking help or discussing mental health challenges.

Depression and suicide are ranked as a leading cause of death among men, with six million males being affected by depression in the United States each year. Data also indicates that men die by suicide at a rate four times higher than women (Mental Health America [MHA], 2020). Mental health among men often goes untreated as they are far less likely to seek treatment than women, with male leaders facing additional pressures.

The Illusion of Invincibility

“Powerful leaders are not exempt from illness by virtue of their position or its influence.”
— McDermott (2008)

Society often glamorizes men in power, expecting them to experience ideals of emotional well-being and an absence of mental health challenges (Cloutier & Barling, 2023). Male leaders are often celebrated for traits that drive them to success – emotional control, perfectionism, obsession with detail, and an unrelenting drive to achieve success.

Beneath the surface, these same qualities can lead to significant internal distress – including depression, anxiety, and feelings of inadequacy. This duality – the outward image of strength versus inner turmoil – is a reality many leaders face but rarely acknowledge openly.

Emotional Disconnect and Its Impact

Many men in power learn to cope with their demands and societal expectations by suppressing their emotions. This disconnect can have serious consequences, making it harder to:

  • Understand and manage their feelings
  • Communicate openly and effectively
  • Build genuine relationships that support emotional well-being
  • Stay true to their values and lead authentically
  • Recognize and prevent burnout

As a result, men often turn to unhealthy coping strategies, including substance use or other addictive patterns to deal with chronic stress or feelings of emptiness that often cloud the enjoyment of accolades or financial achievement. Even at the peak of their careers, many male leaders feel empty or unable to enjoy their success.

Men in power often find themselves in a therapist’s office only as a last resort — when the weight of stressors and emotional isolation leaves them struggling to maintain expected performance or feeling chronically dissatisfied.

Building Emotional Intelligence

For many, seeking therapy is an intimidating step, fearing that exploring their inner world might take away the very traits they credit for their success. Yet, quite the opposite is true: addressing their emotional experiences doesn’t diminish their effectiveness – it enhances it.

By beginning to get in touch with their feelings, male leaders gain a host of skills that improve their well-being and capacity to lead:

  • Emotional Intelligence: Connecting with emotions enhances self-awareness and empathy — two critical qualities of effective leadership. Leaders who understand their emotional triggers can respond thoughtfully rather than react impulsively, fostering a more balanced decision-making style.
  • Enhanced Resilience: Processing emotions helps build psychological resilience, allowing one to navigate high-pressure situations without succumbing to burnout. Understanding and accepting vulnerabilities can help face challenges with clarity and confidence.
  • Authentic Sense of Identity and Self-Worth: Exploring honestly one’s inner world can help develop a stronger sense of self that isn’t only tied to performance or achievement. By exploring values, passions, and vulnerabilities, leaders can build an identity rooted in authenticity, leading to greater fulfillment and balance.
  • Stronger Relationships: Leaders who can express their feelings authentically can create deeper, more meaningful connections with their teams, peers, and families. This not only strengthens workplace collaboration and productivity but helps build a better support system.
  • Improved Creativity and Problem-Solving: Emotional intelligence can allow leaders to approach challenges from new perspectives. By being more aware of their inner experiences, leaders can become better able to access creativity and develop more innovative solutions.

Steps Forward

By prioritizing their emotional well-being and addressing mental health challenges, leaders have the power to shift the narrative around leadership and set a precedent for others to follow. When male leaders take steps on a personal level, they not only improve their own well-being but also have the opportunity to foster a healthier, more productive and sustainable workplace.

Therapy and executive coaching are two powerful ways to begin to transform personal and professional growth or deal with mental health challenges. Key benefits include:

  • Understanding Emotional Patterns: Identifying the root causes of stress or dissatisfaction, paving the way for meaningful change.
  • Developing Coping Strategies: Managing stress more effectively and avoiding unhealthy coping mechanisms, such as substance use.
  • Set and Achieve Goals: Learning to set realistic and meaningful goals that align personal values with professional objectives.
  • Improve Communication Skills: Learning to communicate authentically and effectively, enhancing relationships with teams and peers.
  • Cultivate Balance and Self-Care: Learning to adjust priorities to allow time for regular exercise, restorative sleep, interpersonal connections and hobbies that bring joy and balance.
  • Foster a Support Network: Gaining the interpersonal skills to develop more genuine relationships with trusted friends, family, or mentors who can offer emotional support.
